# AKS — Brand Foundation & Voice

The soul of the site. Everything else — the AI, the 3D, the checkout — is machinery. This is the reason any of it exists. Read this before writing a single line of copy for AKS. Hand it to Claude Code and to any copywriter as the authority on what AKS *means* and how it *speaks*.

---

## 1. The one idea

AKS has two unique selling points, and they are secretly the same idea:

1. **Cut for you, not for a chart.** Nothing exists until you order it. Every piece is cut and stitched to *your* measurements, because standard sizes fit the average of everyone — which is to say, no one.
2. **Made between two worlds.** Eastern craft and soul, a Western quietness of line. Modest by intention, not apology.

Both are about **fit**. One is fit to your *body*. The other is fit to your *two worlds* — the East you come from and the West you live in, which most brands make you choose between. AKS says you don't have to choose. The dress is made to fit you: your measurements *and* your self.

**The line everything collapses into:**

> **Made to fit you. Your measure, your worlds.**

Or, using the name (*aks* = reflection, in Urdu and Persian):

> **A dress that reflects you.**

The name already carries the whole brand in two languages. You rarely have to explain it — you let people notice.

---

## 2. Who she is

One woman, seen from a few angles. Don't write for a "segment" — write for her:

- She might be **abroad** — Pakistani or South Asian, living in London, Toronto, Dubai, New York — dressing between a culture she carries and one she lives in.
- She might be **home in Pakistan**, wanting an Eastern piece with a cleaner, more modern cut.
- She might simply be **any woman tired of clothes that don't fit** — because that part isn't cultural, it's universal.

What they share, and the only thing you need to remember: **she wants something made for her body, that feels like her.** Write to that, and you're writing to all of them at once.

---

## 3. The voice — a friend who sews

Warm, honest, a little witty, never corporate. A friend who sews doesn't *sell* you a dress — she tells you the truth about your shoulders and then makes you something that fits. That's the whole voice.

**Principles**

- **Warm through honesty, not adjectives.** Warmth comes from telling the truth plainly and caring about the outcome — not from "crafted with love." A friend says "standard sizes never fit anyone properly, so let's not pretend." That warmth is earned.
- **Second person, always.** You, your, yours. It's a conversation, not a broadcast.
- **Specific beats poetic.** "Ships in 18–24 days" moves a woman more than "lovingly crafted over time." Detail *is* the emotion.
- **A little wit, lightly.** One dry, human aside is worth more than three exclamation marks. Never try to be funny; just be real, and the warmth reads as charm.
- **Plain is a flex.** You have a real story, so you can afford to drop the marketing words. Brands reach for "luxury" and "elevated" when they have nothing true to say. You have something true.
- **Confident, not boastful.** State what you do and stop. Don't oversell the AI, the craft, or yourself.
- **Modesty in the tone, too.** The voice is calm and uncrowded, the way the clothes are. It doesn't shout, chase, or beg for the click.

**Never say:** luxury, elevated, curated, crafted with love, timeless elegance, for the modern woman, empower(ed), effortless, must-have, unlock, journey, "we're so excited," exclamation-mark enthusiasm, or anything you'd find on a thousand other stores. If a line could belong to any brand, it isn't AKS.

**The test for every line:** *Would a warm, honest friend who sews actually say this out loud?* If it sounds like a marketing team, rewrite it.

## 4. Language — how much Urdu, and where (per-surface recommendation)

You asked me to recommend. The principle: **Urdu and Eastern words are seasoning, not a second menu.** Use them where they add soul or meaning; never where they cost a customer clarity on a functional control. On the Pakistan (`ur`) locale everything is fully Urdu (human-written); the guidance below is for the default English experience.

| Surface | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Logo / wordmark** | Bilingual: `aks` + عکس together. This lockup *is* the East/West thesis — use it everywhere the logo appears. |
| **Home hero** | English, with the meaning of *aks* revealed once, gently ("*aks* — a reflection"). One Urdu word, earning its place. |
| **Atelier / about page** | The richest place for Eastern language. *Karigar* (the artisan), *adda* (the embroidery frame), *aks*. Introduce each naturally, gloss lightly, never with a footnote. This page is where the two worlds are named out loud. |
| **Product & size controls** | English (and `ur` on the Urdu locale). Function first — a woman choosing a sleeve length should never decode a word. Clarity is a form of respect. |
| **Order status / production** | Gentle Eastern texture is lovely here — "your karigar is cutting" reads warmer than "in production," and it's *true*. Use real workshop language for real workshop stages. |
| **Empty & error states** | English, warm, plain. No Urdu needed; this is where you're being helpful, not poetic. |
| **Fabric library** | Eastern fabric names as they truly are — lawn, jamawar, khaddar, organza — no translation, because these are the right words and she likely knows them. |
| **Footer / packaging / labels** | Bilingual marks and a single Urdu line (e.g. عکس) — the physical, keepable surfaces are a good place for the script to live. |

Rule: an Urdu word appears because it's the *truest* word (karigar, jamawar, aks), never as decoration. If English says it better on that screen, use English.

## 5. The copy deck — real lines for the actual screens

Starting points in the AKS voice. Adjust, don't sanitize.

**Home — hero**
> Nothing here exists yet.
> We make each piece after you order it — cut to your measurements, not a chart's.

**Home — the idea, one scroll down**
> *aks* means a reflection.
> Eastern hands, a quieter line. Made to fit your body, and the two worlds you live between.

**Home — how it works (three plain steps)**
> Choose a piece. Give us your measurements. We cut, stitch, and send it — yours, and only yours.

**Collection / listing intro**
> Everything is made to order. Choose what's yours; we'll begin the moment you do.

**Product page — the size choice (invert the default: custom is primary)**
> **Made to your measurements** — the way it should fit.
> *or* — use a standard size (we'll be honest: it won't fit quite like your own).

**Size guide**
> Standard sizes fit the average of everyone, which is to say, no one in particular.
> Here's how each size measures up — but if you want it to truly fit, give us your own.

**Custom size — intro**
> Tell us your measurements and watch the fit change. Take your time — this is the part that makes it yours.

**Custom size — a gentle limit prompt**
> That's an unusual measurement — worth checking the tape once more. We'd rather ask now than take it in later.

**Try it on (Reflection)**
> See it on you, near enough. It's a sketch of the real thing — your dress will be made by hand, so treat this as a good first look.

**Add to cart / cart empty**
> Empty for now. Everything we make begins the moment you choose it.

**Cart — lead time, told honestly**
> Made to order, so it takes a little time — about 18–24 days. Worth the wait for something that's actually yours.

**Checkout — the deposit, in plain words**
> Pay half now to begin, half when it's ready. Once we cut the fabric to your measurements, that first half is committed — because your dress can't become anyone else's.

**Order placed**
> It's begun. We'll cut, stitch, and keep you posted at every step — no need to wonder where it is.

**Order status — the stages, in workshop language**
> Confirmed → Measurements checked → Being cut → With the karigar → Embroidery → Final check → On its way to you

**Atelier / about**
> AKS started with a simple frustration: nothing ever fit.
> So we stopped making clothes for a size and started making them for a person. Every piece is cut after you order it, by hands that have done this a long time — Eastern craft, held to a quieter, modern line. Covered, considered, and made to be yours.

**Account — order history, empty**
> No orders yet. When you place one, it'll live here — every piece we've made for you.

**Error — something failed**
> That didn't go through. Not your fault — try once more, and if it sulks again, message us on WhatsApp and we'll sort it.

**Footer — a single quiet line**
> AKS — عکس. Made to fit you.

## 6. The lexicon

**Words that are ours** (use freely, they carry the brand): *made to order, cut for you, your measurements, karigar, aks / reflection, fit, yours, the two worlds, quiet, considered, by hand, begins when you order.*

**Eastern words we use as-is** (the true words, lightly introduced on first use only): *aks (reflection), karigar (the artisan who makes it), adda (the embroidery frame), jamawar / khaddar / lawn / organza (fabrics).*

**Words we never use** (see §3): luxury, elevated, curated, crafted with love, timeless, effortless, empower, journey, must-have, exclusive, premium, for the modern woman.

---

## 7. How this shows up everywhere (so it's not just words)

The philosophy is only believable if the *site itself* proves it. Copy states it; structure demonstrates it:

- **No inventory, only making.** There is no "in stock." The absence is the argument. Don't add stock counts; add "begins when you order."
- **Custom is the default, standard is the fallback.** The primary button is *made to your measurements.* This one inversion says more than any paragraph.
- **The workshop is visible.** The production timeline the customer watches is live proof her dress didn't exist until she asked. Most brands hide the karigar; AKS shows the hands.
- **The look is the merger.** Bilingual wordmark, Eastern dyes (indigo, madder, zari) in a calm Western editorial layout, modesty shown through restraint and framing. When a visitor can't say *why* it feels both rooted and modern — that's the brand working. Nobody had to write "East meets West" on a banner.
